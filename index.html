01 / A RESEARCH-LED QUESTION

Safety matters.
So does access.

TextNow publicly documents CAPTCHA troubleshooting, Persona identity verification, account restrictions, and recovery paths. Those protections serve real security purposes.

Its account-limitation guidance also acknowledges that legitimate users can be caught in spam filters and points them to support. That makes the measurement question concrete. The scale of any friction—and TextNow’s internal analytics coverage—remain unknown.

Definitions, assumptions & reproducibility

Dataset: 2,400 invented sessions generated with Python seed 20261019. All filtered numbers use the same CSV; there are no real user records.

Outcomes: Verified, Retry needed, Support recovery and Not completed are mutually exclusive categories in this model. Directly verified counts only Verified. Support recovery is reported separately; neither is a production success metric.

Noncompletion: Not completed sessions carrying the invented simulated_legitimate = 1 label ÷ all sessions with that label in the selected segment. This is not a false-positive rate or detection-accuracy estimate.

Retries and resolution: Retry share uses retry_count > 0 ÷ selected sessions. Median resolution uses only Support recovery rows. The model records no recovery-attempt population, so it cannot calculate a recovery success rate.

Modeling limits: Routes are randomly assigned and outcomes use authored probability weights. “No challenge” and “Identity check” are analytical categories, not a reconstruction of TextNow routing or policy. Segment differences reflect those assumptions.

Day-7 return: Included in the CSV but not used as evidence here. It is correlated with outcomes by construction, not an observed retention effect.

Inspect the generator ↗
Important limitation. “Legitimate user” is an invented label in this simulation, not an observable truth in production. Real measurement would need approved proxies, privacy safeguards and human review. The sample proves a method, not a TextNow failure or impact estimate.
03 / WHAT I WOULD TEST

Don't trade safety
for a prettier metric.

A useful pilot would evaluate both sides of the decision: whether legitimate users can finish their journey, and whether the protections still do their job. One-sided success metrics can create the wrong incentives.

01 / DEFINE

Map the journey

Work with Trust & Safety and Product to define events, allowed cohorts, legitimate-user proxies and security guardrails.

02 / BASELINE

Review the friction

Measure completion, retries, recovery outcomes and resolution time by allowed platform and route dimensions. Audit labeling quality and possible false positives before interpreting differences.

03 / VALIDATE

Test carefully

Pilot clearer guidance or a support handoff with an appropriate comparison group. Measure resolution, support burden, and abuse signals together; stop if safety deteriorates or data is unreliable.

Proposed analytical framework only. It is not a recommendation to disable, weaken or bypass verification.
